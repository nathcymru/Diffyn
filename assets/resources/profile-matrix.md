# Combined preference matrix

Reviewed 26 October 2026. These are options to discuss, not conclusions about a person. No diagnosis or health disclosure is required. A self-described neurotype can suggest options; explicit preferences decide what is applied.

| Stated need or optional self-description | Possible support | Preference conflict to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explicit communication; autism | Stable terms, named assumptions, concrete references | Retain requested humour, warmth, technical detail, or creative expression. |
| Attention or initiation; ADHD | Next action first, short groups, clear pause points | Extra bold text, reminders, and questions can increase distraction. |
| Reading fatigue; dyslexia | Short sections, descriptive headings, plain-text choice | Removing terminology can damage accuracy. Excessive emphasis can add clutter. |
| Motor sequencing; dyspraxia | Linear physical steps, named controls, known prerequisites | Too many microsteps may be frustrating for a familiar task. |
| Autism and ADHD; AuDHD | Predictable organization with a concise opening and available detail | Do not assume a fixed balance between novelty and stability. |
| ADHD and dyslexia | Small sections with optional restrained emphasis | Do not enable fragmented word bolding automatically. |
| Autism and low-pressure communication | Clear options with neutral wording | Softened language must not obscure requirements or safety urgency. |
| Dyslexia and dyspraxia | Sequential text with progress labels and minimal navigation | Avoid repeated scrolling between separated instructions. |
| Numeracy support with any profile | Exact value and units plus an optional explanation | Rounding or qualitative labels can distort amounts and risk. |

## Resolve conflicts without collecting diagnoses

1. Preserve the user's original task and essential meaning.
2. Apply explicit preferences supplied for the current task.
3. Offer a neutral format when preferences are absent; do not classify the person from their text.
4. When two preferences genuinely conflict, ask one short relevant question or offer a reversible choice.
5. Let the person change formats, choose more detail, or decline a suggestion.

Support levels, spelling, pace, and vocabulary do not determine intelligence or decision-making capacity. This matrix is a product design aid, not a validated clinical taxonomy.

## Sources

[NICE adult autism guidance](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/CG142/chapter/Recommendations) supports individualized communication and autonomy. [NHS adult dyspraxia guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/developmental-coordination-disorder-dyspraxia-in-adults/) describes practical adjustments. [W3C personalization guidance](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/#support-adaptation-and-personalization) supports user-selected formats. The combinations above are engineering suggestions, not outcomes established by those sources.
