# Combined preference matrix

**Explicit preferences determine what is applied.** The options below support discussion without classifying the person or requiring diagnostic disclosure.

Self-described neurotypes do not change the local tools' settings, and these combinations are engineering suggestions rather than validated clinical presets.

## Individual supports

| Stated need or optional self-description | Support to offer | Preference to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Explicit communication; autism** | Stable terms, named assumptions and concrete references. | Preserve requested humour, warmth, technical detail and creativity. |
| **Attention or initiation; ADHD** | A next action, short groups and visible progress. | Avoid extra reminders or emphasis that the person finds distracting. |
| **Reading fatigue; dyslexia** | Short sections, descriptive headings and a plain-text choice. | Keep precise terminology and avoid excessive visual emphasis. |
| **Motor sequencing; dyspraxia** | Linear actions, named controls and known prerequisites. | Let the person choose useful granularity for familiar tasks. |

**Text alternative:** Offer explicit references, manageable action groups, readable sections or sequential instructions according to the stated need, then check the person's presentation preferences.

## Combined supports

- **Autism and ADHD / AuDHD:** Offer predictable organization with a concise opening and available detail without assuming a fixed preference for novelty or stability.
- **ADHD and dyslexia:** Offer small sections with restrained optional emphasis without automatically enabling fragmented word bolding.
- **Autism and low-pressure communication:** Offer clear, neutral choices while preserving actual requirements and safety urgency.
- **Dyslexia and dyspraxia:** Offer sequential text and progress labels without requiring repeated navigation between distant instructions.
- **Numeracy support with any profile:** Keep exact values and units visible alongside any optional explanation or labelled approximation.

**A display boundary is not a safety assessment:** Confirm whether a procedure can pause before describing a checklist group as a stopping point.

## Resolve a preference conflict

1. **Preserve meaning:** Keep the original task and essential information intact.
2. **Apply explicit choices:** Use preferences supplied for the current task.
3. **Use neutral defaults:** Avoid classifying the person when preferences are absent.
4. **Clarify if needed:** Ask one short relevant question or offer a reversible choice when a consequential conflict remains.
5. **Keep control with the person:** Allow a different format, more detail or no suggestion.

**Support needs are not capacity scores:** Spelling, pace, vocabulary and diagnostic labels do not determine intelligence or decision-making capacity.

## Evidence and limits

Individual communication and autonomy are supported in [nice-cg142](https://github.com/nathcymru/Diffyn/blob/main/docs/bibliography.bib), while practical coordination-related adjustments are described in [nhs-dyspraxia-adults](https://github.com/nathcymru/Diffyn/blob/main/docs/bibliography.bib).

User-selected formats are supported by [w3c-coga-2021](https://github.com/nathcymru/Diffyn/blob/main/docs/bibliography.bib), with broader interpretation and limitations explained in the [research basis](https://github.com/nathcymru/Diffyn/blob/main/docs/research.md).

These sources do not establish the effectiveness of every combined support listed here.
