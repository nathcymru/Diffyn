# Numbers, dates and units

Preserve original values and units. Add explanations alongside them. Never replace an exact value with an unlabelled approximation or an assumed qualitative judgement.

## Derived numbers

Use the numeric tool only for its supported exact operations and validated inputs. Preserve units and denominators. Display any rounding and distinguish the exact result from a decimal rendering. Unsupported conversions need a verified method; do not invent a conversion factor or silently change a currency, dosage or measurement.

A percentage needs a whole and a time period where relevant. Relative and absolute risk are different quantities. “Low”, “warm” and “expensive” need context and should not be substituted automatically. Explanations support understanding, not professional advice.

## Dates and duration

Dates such as 03/04 are ambiguous without a convention. Relative dates need a reference date; times may need a time zone. “Working days” can depend on weekends, holidays and jurisdiction. Keep these unknowns visible and ask when they change an action. Do not claim the numeric tool supplies a calendar or legal-deadline engine.

## Review

Check that amounts, signs, bounds, uncertainty, conditions and genuine urgency survive the final response. Detail comparison can flag changed tokens; it cannot validate the meaning of a calculation, the applicability of a rate or the authority of a deadline.
