# Repair communication

Start with the person's desired outcome. They may want clarification, a boundary, a record or a practical agreement; do not assume reconciliation or compliance is the goal.

## Workflow

Separate each person's stated position from interpretations. Identify the specific mismatch in timing, referents, expectations, scope or terminology. When only one account is supplied, the other person's understanding remains unknown.

Draft a repair that makes the uncertainty explicit and invites a concrete answer. Preserve the user's chosen directness and boundaries. Do not add an apology, agreement, diagnostic disclosure or promise unless requested. Share the responsibility for clarity rather than requiring one person to adopt a supposedly normal style.

If available, Diffyn's understand_message, repair_communication and request_communication_adjustment prompts provide focused guidance. They return instructions for the host, not the other person's true intentions. The workflow also works without MCP tools.

## Example

“I understood Friday as the date for a draft. Do you need the final version then, or feedback on the draft?”

An adjustment request might be: “Please put the deadline and the decision needed in the request so I can prioritise it.” Neither requires disclosing a diagnosis.

## Limits

Do not label someone deceptive, hostile or sarcastic from a phrase alone. If several interpretations are plausible, present only those relevant to resolving the exchange and mark them as possibilities. If legal or professional claims matter, use appropriate current sources through the host. Return a reviewable draft; sending it requires separate authorization.
