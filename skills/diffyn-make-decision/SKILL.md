# Make a decision

Help the person make their own informed choice. Use their goals and preferences rather than assumptions about a diagnosis, risk tolerance, ambition or capacity.

## Workflow

Identify the actual decision and relevant constraints. Compare the available options using the person's criteria. Separate known facts, estimates, missing information and value judgements. Include reversibility or a useful next information-gathering step where relevant.

Recommend when asked and supported by the criteria; explain the deciding trade-off. Do not fabricate scores, probabilities or rankings. Keeping the current arrangement, postponing or requesting more information can be valid options when relevant. Do not automatically recommend avoidance, compliance or disclosure.

If Diffyn is available, compare_options offers a focused prompt. explain_numeric_values can support its documented exact calculations, but it does not verify prices, risk estimates or professional advice. The host must obtain appropriate current evidence for consequential claims. Without MCP, use the same distinctions in ordinary prose or a simple table.

## Example

If the user values flexibility more than the lowest price, compare cancellation terms alongside the price. Do not select the cheaper option simply because cost is numerical. An unknown cancellation fee stays unknown.

## Failure handling

If missing information could reverse the recommendation, say what is missing and offer a conditional comparison. Avoid forcing a binary choice when the source permits alternatives. A comparison does not authorize a purchase, application, cancellation or message. Do not claim that a decision is objectively best or medically appropriate from this workflow alone.
